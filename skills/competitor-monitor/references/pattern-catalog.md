# Pattern catalog

Six named patterns, each requiring two or more corroborating signals.
Patterns are not mutually exclusive — a competitor's deltas can be
consistent with more than one. Every one of these is a correlation the data
supports, not a confirmed read of the competitor's actual strategy.

| Pattern | Signals required | Consistent with | Suggested monitoring posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clearance_push` | price down ≥ threshold, inventory proxy down ≥ threshold, rank improved | Discounting to clear stock while gaining visibility from the resulting sales velocity | Hold price; watch for a restock signal before assuming the clearance is permanent — a real clearance ends when stock runs out |
| `aggressive_ad_push` | rank improved, review velocity up, price roughly flat | Increased ad spend or promotion placement without discounting | Monitor shared-keyword auction pressure; no price action implied |
| `stockout_risk` | inventory proxy down ≥ threshold, price flat or up, rank declining | Running low on stock without needing to discount — the falling rank likely reflects marketplace deprioritization of a low-stock listing, not falling demand | No defensive action needed — a competitor stockout is usually a share opportunity, not a threat. Watch for their restock signal |
| `price_war_signal` | price down ≥ threshold, inventory proxy roughly flat | A price cut that isn't paired with a clearance — could be a temporary promotion or a sustained repricing | Verify it isn't a time-boxed promotion (check listing/promotion signals if available) before matching the price |
| `fading_listing` | rank declining, review velocity down, inventory proxy flat or up | Losing visibility or buyer interest while stock is available — not currently a competitive threat | Low priority for monitoring bandwidth this period |
| `restock_recovery` | inventory proxy up ≥ threshold, price roughly flat | Recovered from a low-stock period, returning to normal competitive pressure | Re-evaluate defensive posture — the stockout-driven opportunity window is closing |

## Why "consistent with," never "is"

Two sellers with identical price/rank/inventory-proxy deltas can have
opposite real reasons — one clearing a warehouse before a lease ends, one
running a scheduled seasonal promotion that looks identical from the outside.
Nothing about these signals lets an outside observer distinguish them. Naming
the pattern that fits and hedging the interpretation is the honest version of
this skill; asserting the competitor's actual motive is not something the
data supports, no matter how clean the pattern match looks.
